The assistant and your shopfront
What it does, exactly
  • It answers in your website chat, in whatever language people write in.
  • Only from what you put in its 57 fields — it asks for 24 to get going: meeting point, times, duration, where it ends, how to spot the guide, rain, children, dogs, accessibility, what to bring, cancellations…
  • What it does not know it hands to you — and logs it in a list sorted by how many times you have been asked, so you know what to answer first.
  • You answer it once and from then on it answers. That question stops reaching you.
  • It knows what is happening in the street: someone who writes “I’m lost” gets the live map, never the guide’s phone number.
  • It authorises nothing only a person can authorise —a minor coming alone, an allergy, a guide dog— and it never quotes places it has not checked.
What it does, exactly
  • Already have a website? Then you do not need this one: Audutor synchronises with whatever site you have. Bookings land here on their own, and if you cancel a departure or the seats run out, your site stops selling them. Set up on day one.
  • Your public page, a front page and one per tour. You do not write it: it comes from the Brain. A field you left empty does not appear — no label, no dash, no "ask us".
  • The free tour is booked right there, against the real capacity. This is the part people get wrong most: for the free tour you need nothing else; "Your paid tour" is only for tours with a price.
  • Each guide's own page, with their QR. The guide shows it at the end of the walk — which is when people ask what else you run — and that recommendation lands on your site, not on the platform. And the analytics tell you which guide brings bookings.
  • Cancel it and THIS page goes dark — along with your guides' QR codes, which point at it. Bookings coming in through your own website keep coming: those do not depend on this add-on.
  • No photos, on purpose. A page with grey gaps where images should be looks worse than one without them. And no AI: these are your words.
  • It goes live when you say so: you choose the address and flip the switch. Two gestures, never a side effect of saving a fact.
What it does, exactly
  • The only thing it opens: selling a tour with a price — on the page Audutor builds you and on your own site, the one you already have. The free tour does not need it.
  • Capacity wins. If three seats are left, the fourth booking is refused on the spot — not afterwards.
  • How it is paid: the traveller books, the seat is held straight away, and to pay they are sent your payment link — your gateway. It is not paid inside the page yet.
  • You place the payment ✓, or whoever you say. Until then the booking is marked "payment to confirm", and nothing counts as paid that a person has not seen.
  • The money goes to your gateway, not ours. No commission per booking.

A €25 ticket through a platform leaves you 17 to 20; through your own page, 25 less whatever your gateway charges. Paying inside the page comes later — that's why it's €19 and not more. Drop it whenever you like.

Platforms and the till
What it does, exactly
  • It signs into your platform dashboards with your own session, on your own computer. Audutor stores no platform password, and it is not going to.
  • It counts: how many people each platform has, and how many places are left. If one has 17 and only 14 reached you, the missing three show up.
  • It closes the departures that are full, dashboard by dashboard — which is what you do by hand today, every time one fills up.
  • Saying it closed is not the same as closed: the order stays at “says it did it” until someone checks, and the green ✓ is only ever put there by a person.
  • If it stops halfway, the order goes back into the queue by itself. It is never assumed good just for sitting there.
  • And while the numbers disagree, GuIA stops quoting places until someone looks.
