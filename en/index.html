A normal day with Audutor

No spreadsheet, no copying bookings across, no “does anyone know where the guide is?”.

8:41 AM
4 bookings land from two different platforms. On their own. Two share a surname, 4 minutes apart: Audutor puts them together — 9 people — and flags the group policy in their confirmation.
9:15 AM
Tomorrow’s reminders go out by SMS. One replies “we’re 5 now” — the booking updates itself and the guide sees it on the manifest. (SMS rather than WhatsApp — it reaches their phone without an app or a data plan.)
10:58 AM
“I’m lost, I can’t find the group.” GuIA checks their code, sends the map with the location the guide shared at the last stop, and leaves a note on the tour. Nobody had to phone anybody.
2:15 PM
End of the free tour: the guide takes the tip. A tap on the link GuIA sends, or the group QR — no amount is ever suggested for them. The review comes with the same tap. Two no-shows land on the board: tomorrow those reminders go out earlier.
11:00 PM
The day settles itself. Tips and ticket sales, channel fees, the guide’s share and the company’s — tour by tour, ready for your accountant. Plus tomorrow’s brief: “2 things to check”.

The assistant and your shopfront
What it does, exactly
  • It answers in your website chat, in whatever language people write in.
  • Only from what you put in its 49 fields: meeting point, times, duration, where it ends, how to spot the guide, how long they wait, what happens if it rains, children, dogs, accessibility, toilets, what to bring, cancellations, how people pay at the end…
  • What it does not know it hands to you — and logs it in a list sorted by how many times you have been asked, so you know what to answer first.
  • You answer it once and from then on it answers. That question stops reaching you.
  • It knows what is happening in the street: someone who writes “I’m lost” gets the live map, never the guide’s phone number.
  • It authorises nothing only a person can authorise —a minor coming alone, an allergy, a guide dog— and it never quotes places it has not checked.
Platforms and the till
What it does, exactly
  • It signs into your platform dashboards with your own session, on your own computer. Audutor stores no platform password, and it is not going to.
  • It counts: how many people each platform has, and how many places are left. If one has 17 and only 14 reached you, the missing three show up.
  • It closes the departures that are full, dashboard by dashboard — which is what you do by hand today, every time one fills up.
  • Saying it closed is not the same as closed: the order stays at “says it did it” until someone checks, and the green ✓ is only ever put there by a person.
  • If it stops halfway, the order goes back into the queue by itself. It is never assumed good just for sitting there.
  • And while the numbers disagree, GuIA stops quoting places until someone looks.
