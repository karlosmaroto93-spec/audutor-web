🤖 The agent that signs into your dashboards off

You can run an agent that signs into your platform dashboards. It runs on your own computer, with your own session: Audutor stores no platform password. It closes full departures, yes — but what earns its keep is that it LOOKS: it counts how many people each platform has on record, so you learn about the bookings that never reached your inbox. And even when it says it has closed one, it still shows here as not closed until you check it yourself.

The five things it does — and they are not worth the same

Count people. It opens your departure on each platform and says how many people they have on record. It touches nothing, and it is the most useful thing it does: it is how you learn about the bookings that never reached your inbox.

Read your listing. It reads what you have published and brings it back, to propose it in the brain. It does not even need to sign into your account for that.

Count reviews. How many sit unanswered. Answering them is yours: writing in your name is not something a program does.

Close. It tries, on the dashboards you tick, one by one. And even when it says it closed one, it still shows here as not closed until you look. It never paints a green ✓.

Reopen. Only what it closed, and only when you ask. It does the dashboard; you pick the moment.

WHAT IT CANNOT DO

✕ Open places on its own, or add capacity.

✕ Hold any password of yours: we never ask for one and never store one.

✕ Know who is coming. Each job tells it the platform, the dashboard and the time. No names, no booking references.

✕ Answer a review or write anything in your name.

✕ Enter a dashboard you have not ticked, or more times a day than you set.

Which dashboards can it enter?

Switching it on grants nothing by itself: you choose dashboard by dashboard. With none ticked, it does nothing.

What it LOOKS at for you

Looking touches nothing, and it is the only thing that can learn about the bookings that never reached your inbox. Try it, then ask GuIA «any places left?» in the chat: it stops giving the number. It can only make it say less, never more.
